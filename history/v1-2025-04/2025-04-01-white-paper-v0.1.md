Abstract

In an age of synthetic content, persuasive language, and rapidly evolving communication tools, the need to detect and interpret rhetorical misdirection is greater than ever. This white paper introduces the Bullshit Compass, a semantic pattern detection system designed to identify epistemically vacuous or misleading language across documents such as corporate presentations, white papers, research reports, and strategic memos. By blending traditional linguistic signals with emerging AI-driven contextual awareness, the system provides interpretable scores and flags to help professionals navigate the increasing complexity of language-based deception.

\---

1\. Introduction: The Problem of Modern Bullshit

Bullshit—defined not as a lie, but as language indifferent to truth (Frankfurt, 1986)—is a defining feature of contemporary professional communication. In boardrooms, white papers, webinars, and decks, language is often optimized for plausibility and influence, not precision or transparency.

This has created a risk landscape where:

Teams are misled by internally generated optimism loops.

Investors rely on style rather than substance.

Policy decisions are shaped by rhetoric rather than evidence.

Traditional tools (fact-checking, grammar-checking, style guides) are insufficient. We need semantic instrumentation—tools that look through the language to evaluate its structure, intent, and epistemic quality.

\---

2\. The Bullshit Compass: Architecture and Philosophy

The Bullshit Compass was built using a hybrid methodology:

Spec-first software design, modeled on the classic Internet RFC process

Integration of philosophical concepts (Frankfurt, Wittgenstein)

Modern machine learning and NLP techniques

Its foundation is a modular scoring system that assigns a composite "Bullshit Score" based on the presence of various rhetorical patterns and anomalies.

\---

3\. Seven Core Indicators of Bullshit

The scoring model evaluates text across seven sub-indices:

1\. Tautological Camouflage Rate (TCR)  
Detects circular statements and empty affirmations.

2\. Peterson Weakness Injector (PWI)  
Flags ideological insertions hidden among tautologies.

3\. Semantic Drift Score (SDS)  
Measures topical inconsistency and argument divergence.

4\. Assertion Ambiguity Ratio (AAR)  
Quantifies vague claims, hedging, and unfalsifiable language.

5\. Buzzword Load Index (BLI)  
Counts jargon use divorced from meaningful definition.

6\. Pseudoscience Rhetoric Marker (PRM)  
Highlights misused scientific terms or magical thinking.

7\. Emotional Persuasion Flags (EPF)  
Tracks emotionally manipulative or tribal language.

Each signal is scored independently (0–100), then aggregated.

\---

4\. Contextual Awareness via Language Games

Building on Wittgenstein’s notion of language games, the Bullshit Compass introduces an 8th indicator:

8\. Language Game Contextualization (LGC)  
Evaluates whether statements fit the expected use of language in a specific rhetorical context. For example, the term "quantum" in a physics report vs. in a wellness brochure.

This enables context-sensitive scoring, reducing false positives in satire, science, or parody.

\---

5\. LLM Integration and Semantic Expansion

To address vocabulary evolution and rhetorical camouflage, the Compass is being upgraded to incorporate:

LLM-based vocabulary expansion (dynamic buzzwords, euphemisms, etc.)

Context flagging prompts for satirical vs. manipulative use

Contrastive scoring: comparing semantically similar documents with different integrity levels

6\. Output & Use Cases

The Compass outputs:

Composite Bullshit Score (0–100)

Radar chart across 7–8 axes

Annotated text with flags

Optional rewrite suggestions

Use Cases:

Research integrity audits

Due diligence scans in M\&A

Executive memo reviews

AI-generated content QC

Journalism and fact-checking augmentation
