Summary of the Finger Spider Semantic Tools Project

Prepared by: Rob Tyrie & ChatGPT  
Date: April 2025

\---

1\. Naming & Conceptual Genesis

We began by naming the platform Finger Spider, a nod to the original finger protocol (RFC 742\) from the early internet, combining the ideas of querying identity, forensic detection, and pattern sensing.

Two core tools emerged as flagship products:

Fingerprint Spider: Tracks semantic reuse and linguistic fingerprints.

Bullshit Compass: Measures epistemic integrity through rhetorical pattern analysis.

\---

2\. Platform Architecture

We developed a full Platform Canvas, identifying:

Stakeholders: Tool creators and users

Revenue models: SaaS, consulting, licensing

Capabilities: Modular spiders, semantic search, scorecards, document annotations

We also defined brand architecture for future spiders (e.g., Trust Weaver, Obfuscation Oracle).

\---

3\. Narrative & Philosophy

We authored rich, metaphoric stories explaining the name and function of each product:

Finger Spider connects the earliest querying protocols with modern semantic detection.

Bullshit Compass builds on Frankfurt, Pennycook, and linguistic satire to measure persuasive nonsense.

\---

4\. RFC Authoring

We wrote two formal documents in IETF RFC style:

RFC FS01 – Fingerprint Spider: A Protocol for Semantic Reuse Detection

RFC FS02 – The Bullshit Compass: A Protocol for Epistemic Pattern Detection

These documents include architecture, use cases, scoring systems, and references.

\---

5\. Commentary & Manifesto

We created a public commentary preface to RFC FS02 in the spirit of open-source RFCs.

We also wrote a Medium-style essay:  
“The Forgotten Power of Starting with the Spec”, comparing our process to historical RFCs (HTTP, Email, Finger) and championing "spec-before-code" as a better model for semantic tools.
